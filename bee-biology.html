Meet the family

The queen

The colony’s principal egg-layer. Her reproductive role supports the next generation.

The workers

Female bees that care for brood, maintain the nest, defend it and gather resources.

The drones

Male bees whose reproductive role is mating with virgin queens.

Egg → larva → pupa → adult

“Brood” means the developing young. The egg hatches into a larva; after growth and feeding, the bee develops through the pupal stage and eventually emerges as an adult. Development time differs among queens, workers and drones.

How does a larva become a queen?

Female larvae can develop into workers or queens. Nutrition during early development is central: queen-destined larvae receive a rich, sustained diet of royal jelly. Colonies raise replacement queens during swarming, supersedure or an emergency loss of their queen.

A fascinating genetic difference

Honey bees usually develop as females from fertilized eggs, with two chromosome sets, and as male drones from unfertilized eggs, with one set. This is called haplodiploidy. Because a queen mates with multiple drones, the workers in a colony can have different fathers.

Food is more than honey

Nectar supplies carbohydrates; pollen supplies nutrients needed for brood production. A full-looking honey frame does not tell the whole story of colony nutrition. The availability of flowers helps shape how quickly a colony can grow.
