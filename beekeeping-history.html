Beekeeping, then & now

Beekeepers have been learning from bees for generations. Here are a few changes in how we keep them.

Long before the modern hive

Working with fixed comb

Beekeepers used containers such as pottery, woven skeps, wooden boxes and hollow logs. Comb fixed to the container was difficult to remove and inspect. Harvesting and examining a colony were very different from lifting individual frames today.

The 1600s

Honey bees arrive in colonial North America

The western honey bee, Apis mellifera, is not native to the Americas. Historical records describe honey bee colonies shipped from England to Virginia in 1622. Native bees and other pollinators were already here; honey bee history is only one part of pollinator history.

The nineteenth century

Movable frames change the work

Movable-comb equipment made it possible to examine brood and food without cutting the entire nest apart. This helped make regular colony inspection and modern honey handling practical.

1987 onward in the United States

A new parasite changes the routine

Varroa’s arrival in the United States in 1987 added a major challenge. Modern care must account for mites and the viruses associated with them, alongside nutrition, pesticide exposure and other stresses. A colony can look busy while its health is deteriorating.
