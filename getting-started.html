Your first season starts here.

Prepare for the bees before the bees arrive—with local help for your area.

1. Find a local learning partner

Contact your county extension office and a nearby beekeeping association or experienced mentor. For Liberal and the surrounding area, ask specifically about High Plains forage, wind exposure, water and winter preparation. A class plus hands-on experience will help you interpret what you see.

2. Check your location

Confirm current city, county and property requirements before installing hives. Plan access, water and a flight path considerate of neighbors. Ask a local mentor to assess the site; a map alone cannot reveal all the practical issues.

3. Budget for care, not just a hive

Allow for protective gear, a smoker, hive tools, appropriate boxes and frames, a feeder, a mite-monitoring kit and ongoing supplies. Learn to handle bees with supervision and suitable protective equipment.

4. Choose a reputable source of bees

A package begins without established comb or brood. A nucleus colony includes established frames and developing bees. Ask about queen status, pest management and equipment history. Learn the first inspections and feeding needs before pickup day.

5. Make a first-year care plan

Plan inspections, monitoring and food checks with a mentor. Building a healthy colony may take priority over harvesting honey. Keep a record from the first day and adapt it to your actual conditions.
