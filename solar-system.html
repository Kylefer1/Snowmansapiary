Reading the map

Space is mostly space.

The planets follow their calculated positions for the selected instant. Orbit lines trace a full revolution; they are not circular animations. Choose any planet above to bring it into view.

One astronomical unit (AU) is 149,597,870.7 kilometers, about the average Earth to Sun distance. Even at this scale, the planets would be too small to see, so their markers are enlarged. Keyboard users can focus the map and use the arrow keys to pan, + and − to zoom, and Home to reset.

How the positions are calculated

This educational map uses Astronomy Engine 2.1.19, with VSOP87-based planetary calculations designed for about one arcminute of accuracy. It is a calculated view of the solar system, not a live telescope image.

The view looks down from the north side of the fixed J2000 ecliptic plane. Planets are shown at the same instant, without correcting for the time their light takes to reach Earth. Their height above or below the plane is flattened in the map and included in the details. The Earth to Jupiter line shows their geometric separation.

For research, navigation or high-precision ephemerides, use NASA JPL Horizons. This map accepts dates from 1800 through 2050.
